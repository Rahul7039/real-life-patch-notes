# Candidates — Real-Life Patch Notes: The Comedy Changelog

Scored against the published judge brief, most important first:
1. single-line patch note about a real-life / human-biology bug, reads as comedy
2. genuinely funny on first read, rests on a real comedic idea
3. reads like an actual release note (version number, terse engineering register, fix stated as shipped)
4. names a specific, recognisable bug — reader winces and laughs at once
5. uses its 160 characters well, no padding

## 1. ★ WINNER (114 chars)
> v1.9.3: fixed a bug where the human brain filed every embarrassing memory under 'urgent' and replayed it at 3 a.m.

Rationale: the strongest. A universally recognised bug (3 a.m. cringe replays) framed as a filing/priority-queue defect — "filed under 'urgent'" is a genuine engineering metaphor, not just the word "bug" dropped in. Dry release-note register, version number, fix stated as shipped. Every word works; the punchline lands on first read and the reader winces and laughs at once.

## 2. (103 chars)
> v2.0.1: fixed a bug where sneezing in a quiet room triggered a 10-second apology loop in nearby humans.

Rationale: good specific daily-life bug, "apology loop" is a nice software metaphor, clean register. Weaker than #1: the joke is observational rather than a wince — sneezing is less personally painful than reliving embarrassment at 3 a.m.

## 3. (101 chars)
> v3.1.4: fixed a bug where the stomach growled loudest in silent rooms, libraries, and job interviews.

Rationale: specific, recognisable, escalating list (silent rooms → libraries → job interviews) is a solid joke structure. Weaker than #1: no software metaphor — it reads as a funny observation dressed as a patch note rather than a bug with a mechanism.

## 4. (94 chars)
> v1.4.7: fixed a bug where yawning spread to nearby users even when they had slept eight hours.

Rationale: "spread to nearby users" is a good networked-systems framing of contagious yawning, and "even when they had slept eight hours" sharpens it. Weaker than #1: yawning is mild — no real wince, and the punchline is gentler.

## 5. (103 chars)
> v2.7.2: fixed a bug where the brain auto-played the same song on loop for 48 hours with no skip button.

Rationale: earworm as a stuck media player with "no skip button" is a strong software metaphor and a specific, widely felt bug. Weaker than #1: slightly more setup words for a slightly smaller emotional payoff; the 3 a.m. memory hits harder.

## 6. (93 chars)
> v5.0.0: fixed a bug where hair grew everywhere except the one place users actually wanted it.

Rationale: terse, specific, instantly recognisable (balding + body hair). Weaker than #1: the joke is a one-beat observation with no mechanism or software metaphor — it leans on the premise alone.

## 7. (85 chars)
> v1.3.9: fixed a bug where the nose itched only when both hands were covered in paint.

Rationale: funny, specific, terse, and the condition is precisely stated. Weaker than #1: a minor annoyance rather than a wince-worthy bug, and the shortest — it leaves character budget unused without adding a punchline.

## 8. (92 chars) — the kit's example, NOT submitted
> v2.4.1: fixed a bug where the left ear rendered audio 40ms late after a full night of sleep.

Rationale: listed only as the kit's own example for comparison; not a candidate for submission. Decent register but the joke is thin — a 40ms delay is barely a bug, and the punchline never lands.
